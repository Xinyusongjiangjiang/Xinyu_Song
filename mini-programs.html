Policy Proposal: Bringing AI Mini-Programs into Public Schools

While volunteering as a teacher, I noticed that some students had limited access to English-learning resources. I wanted to build a small AI mini-program to support them, but policy restrictions kept it out of schools. That experience taught me that educational technology must balance usefulness with school rules, privacy, teacher workload, and student safety.

Policy Proposal AI Mini-Programs in Public Schools ↗
01

Observation

Useful tools cannot enter schools casually.

The idea started with something I saw while teaching: students wanted to learn English, but the resources around them were limited. I wanted to build a small AI tool that could give them additional practice and support. When policy restrictions prevented the tool from entering schools, I began to understand that educational technology is never only a technical question.

02

Policy question

Safety and usefulness have to be designed together.

After I learned that my mini-program could not enter schools, I started looking into the policies behind that decision. I came to understand that digital tools may look convenient, but once they enter a school, they also enter a system of rules, privacy concerns, teacher workload, and student protection. This made me curious: what kind of policy can protect students' safety while still allowing useful educational tools to serve them better?

03

Why it matters

Technology needs a route into real learning.

As technology develops quickly, useful tools could help reduce educational inequality by bringing better support into learning. But schools need clear policies for how these tools are introduced, reviewed, protected, and evaluated.

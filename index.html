My Story

My story begins between two places: a village hometown in Beijing and the city where I grew up. That distance shaped how I noticed education, responsibility, and the meaning of returning to help.

Childhood

Between a Village Hometown and an Urban Childhood

My hometown is a village in Beijing, but I grew up in the city. Moving between these two worlds made the urban-rural gap feel personal rather than abstract.

Family education

Learning to Give Back

My parents wanted me to see a larger world and experience better education so I could keep growing. But they also taught me that after seeing more, I should not forget where I came from.

Family tradition

My Father’s Example of Giving Back

During the pandemic, my father donated daily necessities to our village. I grew up seeing that giving back is not just an idea or a slogan; it is a practical responsibility when you are able to help.

Teaching practice

Returning to Teach at My Hometown Primary School

When I later taught at the primary school near my hometown, the differences I had sensed growing up became visible in classrooms: in resources, teaching conditions, and what students could receive every day.

Research question

Turning Experience Into Inquiry

Those experiences led me to study education finance, urban-rural inequality, teaching methods, school systems, and the policies that might help useful tools reach students safely.

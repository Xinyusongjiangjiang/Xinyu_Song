Starting point

Classrooms

My classroom story has two sides: my life as a high school student, and my experience teaching students in a classroom near my hometown.

Part 01

High School Life

I started high school in a public school and was fairly quiet. After transferring to an international school, I gradually realized that its diverse and more personalized environment suited the way I learned and grew. Feeling understood and supported helped me become more confident and more willing to express myself.
I began to take part in activities that I would once have avoided. Whether working with classmates, speaking in front of others, or trying something completely new, I learned that confidence often comes from participating before feeling fully ready.
This experience also changed the way I see a classroom. I became more aware of how much a supportive environment can shape a student's willingness to ask questions, make mistakes, and believe that they belong.

Part 02

Teaching Practice

I went to a primary school near my hometown to volunteer as a teacher, and I genuinely enjoyed the process of teaching. At the same time, I saw that having access to school does not necessarily mean having access to strong educational resources. The students had fewer materials and fewer opportunities, but their curiosity and desire to learn were unmistakable.

One moment I will never forget was teaching a young girl addition and subtraction. We counted on her fingers and worked through the same problem again and again. When she finally solved it on her own, I felt a sense of accomplishment that was difficult to describe. I had not simply taught her an answer; I had helped her reach it herself.
